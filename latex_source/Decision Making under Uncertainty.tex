\frame{
\frametitle{Expected Utility}



John \textbf{von Neumann} and Oskar \textbf{Morgenstern} have determined conditions under which agents' preferences for risky payments (lotteries) can be described by an {\bf \red expected utility function} such that 

$$E[u(\tilde{a})]= \sum_{n=1}^N \pi_n u(a_n)$$

with $u$ {\red \bf concave and increasing}, $u' > 0$ and $u'' < 0$.

\vspace{0.5cm}
More generally, if a risky payment $\tilde{a}$ has a distribution $d\Phi$,

$$E[u(\tilde{a})] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} u(a)d\Phi(a)$$


\textbf{Bibliography.} The axiomatic approach behind the theory of the expected utility function are developed by von Neumann and Morgenstern in their book \textit{Theory of Games and Economic Behavior} (1947). 


}


\section{Risk Aversion}

\subsection*{}

\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion}

Under these conditions, agents are risk averse. In particular, with \textbf{Jensen inequality} for a concave function, we have 

$$E[u(\tilde{a})] \leq u(E[\tilde{a}]).$$

Agents would prefer to obtain the mean of payment with certainty rather than the risky payment.

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{How to quantify risk aversion?}

%\pause
\vspace{0.3cm}

The level of concavity, measured by the second derivative, $u''$, \textbf{is not} a good measure. Indeed, if
$$v = \alpha u +\beta, \text{with} \ \alpha > 1$$

$v$ generates the same relation of order among risky payments than $u$ does. Yet $v'' < u'' <0$.

$\Rightarrow$ A measure of risk aversion must be invariant with any affine transformation of the utility function.

}


\frame{
\frametitle{Measuring Risk Aversion}

There are two classic measures of risk aversion


$$R_A(y) = - \frac{u''(y)}{u'(y)} = \textbf{coefficient of absolute risk aversion}$$

and

$$R_R(y) = - \frac{yu''(y)}{u'(y)} = \textbf{coefficient of relative risk aversion}$$

where $y$ is the payment (the income) of the agent.

\vspace{0.5cm}

Both are positive: $R_A(y) > 0, R_R(y) >0$.

}



\frame{
\frametitle{Interpreting Risk Aversion}


Consider a risk averse agent who receives a risky payment $y + \tilde{\varepsilon}$, with mean $y$ and variance $Var[\tilde{\varepsilon}] = \sigma^2$.
\vspace{0.5cm}

The \textbf{certainty equivalent} of this payment is $y-\Delta$ such that  
%%
$$u(y-\Delta) = E[u(y+\varepsilon)]$$

\vspace{0.5cm}

$\Delta$ is a \textbf{risk premium}: How much the agent is willing to reduce his mean payment to get rid of uncertainty. 



}


\frame{
\frametitle{Interpreting Risk Aversion}



Assuming that $\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$ are small,  $\Delta <<1$ and $\sigma^2 << 1$, give an approximation of $\Delta$. 

%\vspace{5cm}

$$u(y-\Delta) \approx u(y) - \Delta u'(y) $$ 
$$E[u(y+\varepsilon)]  \approx  E[u(y)] + \underbrace{E[\varepsilon u'(y)]}_{=u'(y)E[\varepsilon] = 0} + \frac{1}{2}E[\varepsilon^2 u''(y)] = u(y) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} u''(y)$$


$$\Rightarrow \ \ \Delta = - \frac{u''(y)}{u'(y)}\frac{\sigma^2}{2} = R_A(y) \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$$


}





\frame{
\frametitle{Interpreting Risk Aversion}



When the risky payment is $y + y\tilde{\varepsilon}$, define $\delta$ as
$$u(y-\delta y) = E[u(y+ y\varepsilon)].$$
Similarly, one can give a first order approximation of $\delta$ under the assumption $\delta <<1$ and $\sigma^2 << 1$ (cf Problem Set).


$$u(y-\delta y)) \approx u(y) - \delta y u'(y) $$ 
$$E[u(y+ y\varepsilon)]  \approx   u(y) + \frac{y^2 \sigma^2}{2} u''(y)$$
%
$$\Rightarrow \ \ \delta = - \frac{yu''(y)}{u'(y)}\frac{\sigma^2}{2}= R_R(y) \frac{\sigma^2}{2}$$



\vspace{0.3cm}

Whether uncertainty is absolute or relative (w.r.t $y$), the certainty equivalent depends on the coefficient of absolute or relative risk aversion.
}






\frame{
\frametitle{Usual Utility Functions}


\begin{itemize}

\item CARA (``Constant Absolute Risk Aversion")

$$u(y) = - \frac{1}{\nu}e^{-\nu y}$$

\item CRRA (``Constant Relative Risk Aversion")

$$u(y) = \frac{y^{1-\gamma }}{1-\gamma}$$

and, in addition, $u(y) = \ln(y)$ for $\gamma=1$.
\item[]
\item What are $R_A(y)$ in the CARA case, and $R_R(y)$ in the CRRA case?

\end{itemize}


}


\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Neutrality}

When $u$ is affine, the agent is risk neutral:

$$u(y) = \alpha y  +\beta$$

He only cares about his expected revenue. 

\vspace{0.5cm}

Specifically,

$$R_A(y) =R_R(y) =0 \ \forall y$$ 

}



\section{Portfolio Choice}

\subsection*{}


\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion and Portfolio Choice}


Consider an agent with an expected utility function à la von Neumann-Morgenstern, who invests at $t=0$ and, at $t=1$, receives the pay-off of its investment and ``consume" it (get the utility out of the payment).

\vspace{0.3cm}

The agent can invest his initial wealth $y_0$ in the following way:
\begin{itemize}
\item invests an amount $w$ in a risky asset (the S\&P 500 for instance) with random net return $\tilde{r}$,
\item and the rest in the risk free asset with (certain) net return, $r_f$.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3cm}

His final wealth $\tilde{y}_1$ at  $t=1$:
\begin{align*}
\tilde{y}_1 & = (y_0 - w)\times (1+r_f) + w\times (1+\tilde{r})  \\
 &= y_0 \times (1+r_f) + w\times (\tilde{r}- r_f)
\end{align*}


}


\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion and Portfolio Choice}

Objective function of the agent:

$$\max_{w} U(w) = \max_{w} E[u(\tilde{y}_1)] = \max_{w} E[u(y_0 (1+r_f) + w (\tilde{r}- r_f) )]$$


\vspace{0.3cm} 

Unconstrained optimization of a concave function, 

$$u'' < 0 \Rightarrow U''(w) = E[(\tilde{r}- r_f)^2 u''(y_0 (1+r_f) + w (\tilde{r}- r_f))] <0$$

 
\vspace{0.3cm}

The optimal amount $w^*$ is given by the first order condition:

$$U'(w) = 0 $$





}

\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion and Portfolio Choice}


First order condition:

$$E[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(y_0 (1+r_f) + w^* (\tilde{r}- r_f) )]=0$$

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textit{Remark:} Unconstrained optimization implies that short-selling ($w < 0$) or leverage ($w > y_0$) are possible. 

\vspace{0.5cm}


%\pause


\textbf{Proposition 1.} When $u$ is increasing and concave, $u' > 0,  \ u'' <0$, then,
\begin{align*}
w^* > 0 & \Leftrightarrow E[\tilde{r}] > r_f \\
w^* = 0 & \Leftrightarrow E[\tilde{r}] = r_f \\
w^* < 0 & \Leftrightarrow E[\tilde{r}] < r_f
\end{align*}

\textbf{Implication.} Whenever the expected return is larger than the risk free rate, it is always optimal to invest in (al least a little).

}



\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion and Portfolio Choice}


\textbf{Proof.} Define $F(w)$ as
$$F(w) = E[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(y_0 (1+r_f) + w (\tilde{r}- r_f))]$$
$w^*$ is such that $F(w^*)=0$
\vspace{0.5cm}

Derivative of $F$:

$$F'(w) = E[(\tilde{r}- r_f)^2 u''(y_0 (1+r_f) + w (\tilde{r}- r_f))] <0$$


\vspace{0.5cm}

Value of $F$ in $0$:
$$F(0) = E[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(y_0 (1+r_f))] = \underbrace{u'(y_0 (1+r_f))}_{>0} \times (E[\tilde{r}]- r_f)$$ 

}

\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion and Portfolio Choice}


If the investor is risk neutral:

$$u(y) = \alpha y + \beta$$

%\vspace{5cm}
\vspace{0.5cm}

%\pause 


$$\max_{w} E[u(\tilde{y}_1)] \Leftrightarrow \max_{w} y_0 (1+r_f) + w (E[\tilde{r}]- r_f).$$

Then,

\begin{align*}
E[\tilde{r}] > r_f  \Rightarrow w^* = +\infty \\
E[\tilde{r}] < r_f  \Rightarrow w^* = -\infty
\end{align*}



}










\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion and Portfolio Choice}

Same problem, different formulation: The agent buys $z$ units of risky assets, at price $p$, with pay-off at $t=1$ equal to $\tilde{a}$.
%\pause
\begin{align*}
\tilde{y}_1 & = (y_0 - p.z)\times (1+r_f) + z\times \tilde{a} \\
&= y_0 \times (1+r_f) + z\times (\tilde{a}- (1+r_f)p) 
\end{align*}
%
First order condition:
$$E[(\tilde{a}- (1+r_f)p) u'(y_0 (1+r_f) + z^* (\tilde{a}- (1+r_f)p))]=0$$
$$z^* > 0  \Leftrightarrow \frac{E[\tilde{a}]}{1+r_f} > p$$
%
Consistent with Proposition 1 because:
$$1+ E[\tilde{r}]= \frac{E[\tilde{a}]}{p}$$


}








\section{Initial Wealth}
\subsection*{}

\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion, Portfolio and Initial Wealth}

Let $w^*(y_0)$ the solution of

$$\max_{w} E[u(y_0 (1+r_f) + w (\tilde{r}- r_f))], \ \text{when} \ E[\tilde{r}] > r_f$$
which implies $w^* >0$.

\vspace{0.5cm}


\textbf{Proposition 2.} If $u$ is such that
\begin{align*}
R_{A}'(y) < 0 & \Rightarrow \frac{dw^*}{dy_0} > 0 \\
R_{A}'(y) = 0 & \Rightarrow \frac{dw^*}{dy_0} = 0  \\
R_{A}'(y) > 0 & \Rightarrow \frac{dw^*}{dy_0} < 0 
\end{align*}


}

\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion, Portfolio and Initial Wealth}

\textbf{Proof.} Assume that $R_{A}'(y) < 0$. By taking the derivative of the first order condition with respect to $y_0$ we get:
%\vspace{5cm}
$$E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f)\left(1+r_f + \frac{dw^*}{dy_0} (\tilde{r}- r_f)\right) u''(\tilde{y}_1)\right]=0$$

\begin{align*}
\frac{dw^*}{dy_0} \overbrace{E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f)^2 u''(\tilde{y}_1)\right]}^{<0} &= (1+r_f)E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f)(- u''(\tilde{y}_1))\right] \\
& = (1+r_f) \underbrace{E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1)R_{A}(\tilde{y}_1) \right]}_{=H}
\end{align*}

 
\begin{align*}
H =& \ E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1)R_{A}(\tilde{y}_1)|\tilde{r}< r_f \right]P[\tilde{r}< r_f] \\
& \ + E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1)R_{A}(\tilde{y}_1)|\tilde{r} > r_f \right]P[\tilde{r}> r_f] \\
\leq &  \ E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1)R_{A}(y_0 (1+r_f))|\tilde{r}< r_f \right]P[\tilde{r}< r_f] \\
& \ + E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1)R_{A}(y_0 (1+r_f))|\tilde{r} > r_f \right]P[\tilde{r}> r_f] \\
\leq & \ R_{A}(y_0 (1+r_f)) E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1) \right] = 0
\end{align*}


}



\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion, Portfolio and Initial Wealth}

Let $\eta(y_0)$ the elasticity of $w^*(y_0)$:

$$\eta(y_0) = \frac{d \ln(w^*(y_0))}{d \ln (y_0)}$$


\vspace{0.5cm}


\textbf{Proposition 3.} If $u$ is such that
\begin{align*}
R_{R}'(y) < 0 & \Rightarrow \eta(y_0) > 1 \\
R_{R}'(y) = 0 & \Rightarrow \eta(y_0) = 1  \\
R_{R}'(y) > 0 & \Rightarrow \eta(y_0) < 1 
\end{align*}


}



\frame{
\frametitle{Risk Aversion, Portfolio and Initial Wealth}

\textbf{Proof.} Let $w^* = e^{\theta^*}$ and $y_0 = e^{\gamma_0}$, 
%\vspace{5cm}
$$\eta = \frac{d\theta^*}{d \gamma_0} .$$ 

 By taking the derivative of the first order condition with respect to $\gamma_0$ we get:

$$E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f)\left((1+r_f)y_0 + \frac{d\theta^*}{d \gamma_0} w^* (\tilde{r}- r_f)\right) u''(\tilde{y}_1)\right]=0$$

\begin{align*}
\left(\frac{d\theta^*}{d\gamma_0} -1\right)w^* \overbrace{E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f)^2 u''(\tilde{y}_1)\right]}^{<0} &= E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f)\tilde{y}_1(- u''(\tilde{y}_1))\right] \\
& =  E\left[(\tilde{r}- r_f) u'(\tilde{y}_1)R_{R}(\tilde{y}_1) \right]
\end{align*}

 
}
