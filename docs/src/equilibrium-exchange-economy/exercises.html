Practice Problems

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1

Adam and Bob are the only two individuals in an island. Adam has been able to collect 10 coconuts and 5 fish, while Bob has 5 coconuts and 10 fish. Both individuals have the same utility function over coconut and fish consumption bundles represented by the following Constant Elasticity of Substitution (CES) utility function: \[ u(X_c, X_f) = \left( X_c^{\frac{\sigma - 1}{\sigma}} + X_f^{\frac{\sigma - 1}{\sigma}} \right)^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma - 1}}, \qquad \sigma=2, \] where \(X_c\) is the number of coconuts and \(X_f\) the number of fish.

  1. Compute the Marshallian demand functions for coconuts and fish for both Adam and Bob as a function of prices.
  2. Compute the equilibrium prices of coconuts and fish in this economy.
  3. Compute the equilibrium consumption bundles for both Adam and Bob.
  4. For an arbitrary level \(\bar{u}\) of utility, compute the Hicksian demand functions for both Adam and Bob as a function of prices and utility.
  5. Compute the expenditure function as a function of prices and utility.
  6. Show that Marshallian and Hicksian demands coincide when utility is set to the Marshallian utility level.

Exercise 2

Only one hospital in a country provides medical services. Cities have preferences over medical services \(X\) and cash \(C\): \[ U(X,C) = X^{\frac{1}{2}} C^{\frac{1}{2}}. \] The government has allocated \(1\) million services to city 1 and \(2\) million to city 2. Cities have no cash. A policy lets cities sell medical services to outside buyers and receive 80% of the market price per service. Normalize the price of cash to \(1\) and let the (producer) price of services be \(p_X\). Compute each city’s optimal \((X,C)\) after the policy.

Exercise 3

In the market for tobacco (\(X_t\)) and alcohol (\(X_a\)), Carol and Dave have utilities \[ U_C(X_t, X_a) = X_t^{\frac{1}{3}} X_a^{\frac{2}{3}},\qquad U_D(X_t, X_a) = X_t^{\frac{2}{3}} X_a^{\frac{1}{3}}. \] Endowments: Carol \((4,1)\), Dave \((1,4)\).

  1. Compute Marshallian demands.
  2. Find equilibrium prices.
  3. Compute equilibrium consumption bundles.
  4. Suppose a 20% consumption tax on tobacco (buyers pay \((1+\tau)p_t\) with \(\tau=0.2\); sellers receive \(p_t\)).
      1. Characterize an equilibrium with the tax and lump-sum transfers \((T_C,T_D)\) such that markets clear.
      1. Give one revenue-neutral example with \(T_C+T_D\) equal to tax revenue and compute the resulting prices and allocations.
      1. If the government rebated revenue equally, what are the transfers?

Exercise 4

Apples (\(X_A\)) and pears (\(X_P\)) are traded by Eve and Frank. Block pricing applies to purchases: first \(\bar{A}\) apples at price \(\bar p_a\), additional apples at \(\bar p_a/2\); analogously, first \(\bar P\) pears at price \(\bar p_p\), additional pears at \(\bar p_p/2\). Eve’s endowment \((\omega_{EA},\omega_{EP})\), Frank’s \((\omega_{FA},\omega_{FP})\). Both have Cobb–Douglas utility \[ U(X_A,X_P)=X_A^\alpha X_P^{1-\alpha},\qquad \alpha\in(0,1). \]

  1. Write each consumer’s piecewise-linear budget and the corresponding Marshallian demand case by case.
  2. State market-clearing conditions and characterize equilibrium prices.
  3. Characterize equilibrium allocations.
  4. Comparative statics: how do equilibrium prices/allocations change as \(\bar A\) and \(\bar P\) increase?

Exercise 5

Two-good \((1,2)\), two-consumer \((A,B)\) exchange economy with initial allocations \[ \omega_A=(4,2),\qquad \omega_B=(2,4), \] total endowments \((6,6)\). Both have \(U(X_1,X_2)=X_1^\alpha X_2^{1-\alpha}\).

  1. Draw an Edgeworth box (\(6\times 6\)) and locate the initial endowment.
  2. Draw budget lines through the endowment for each consumer.
  3. Draw indifference curves for \(A\) and discuss their shape.
  4. What is the slope of a budget line?
  5. Compute equilibrium relative prices and show the equilibrium allocation.

Solutions

Solution to Exercise 1

0) Useful identities for \(\sigma=2\)

With \(\sigma=2\), set \(\rho=\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}=\tfrac12\) and write \[ U(X_c,X_f)=\big(X_c^{\rho}+X_f^{\rho}\big)^{1/\rho}=(X_c^{1/2}+X_f^{1/2})^2, \] which is homogeneous of degree 1 (so homothetic). The MRS is \[ \text{MRS}_{c,f}=\frac{\partial U/\partial X_c}{\partial U/\partial X_f} =\frac{X_c^{\rho-1}}{X_f^{\rho-1}}=\left(\frac{X_f}{X_c}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}. \]

Let prices be \((p_c,p_f)\) and income \(I\).

1) Marshallian demands

FOC at an interior optimum: \(\text{MRS}_{c,f}=\frac{p_c}{p_f}\), i.e. \[ \left(\frac{X_f}{X_c}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{p_c}{p_f}\quad\Rightarrow\quad X_f=X_c\left(\frac{p_c}{p_f}\right)^2. \] Budget: \(p_c X_c+p_f X_f=I\). Substitute and solve for \(X_c, X_f\): \[ X_c=\frac{I\,p_f}{p_c(p_c+p_f)},\qquad X_f=\frac{I\,p_c}{p_f(p_c+p_f)}. \] These hold for any consumer with income \(I\). Hence - Adam: \(I_A=10p_c+5p_f\), - Bob: \(I_B=5p_c+10p_f\), and plug into the same formulas.

2) Equilibrium prices

Normalize \(p_c=1\), let \(p_f=p\). Total endowments: \((15,15)\). Aggregate coconut demand is \[ X_{c,A}+X_{c,B} =\frac{(10+5p)p}{1+p}+\frac{(5+10p)p}{1+p} =\frac{(15+15p)p}{1+p}. \] Market clearing \(X_{c,\cdot}=15\) gives \[ \frac{(15+15p)p}{1+p}=15 \;\Rightarrow\; p^2=1 \;\Rightarrow\; p=1. \] Thus \(p_c=p_f=1\).

3) Equilibrium allocations

With \(p_c=p_f=1\), \(I_A=15\), \(I_B=15\) and \[ X_{c,i}^{M}=X_{f,i}^{M}=\frac{I_i}{2}=\frac{15}{2}=7.5,\quad i\in\{A,B\}. \]

4) Hicksian demands (any prices)

Minimize \(p_c X_c+p_f X_f\) s.t. \((X_c^{1/2}+X_f^{1/2})^2\ge \bar u\). Equivalently \(X_c^{1/2}+X_f^{1/2}\ge \bar u^{1/2}\). Standard CES duality for \(\sigma=2\) yields the Hicksian (compensated) demands \[ X_c^{h}(p,\bar u)=\bar u\cdot \frac{4\,p_f^2}{(p_c+p_f)^2},\qquad X_f^{h}(p,\bar u)=\bar u\cdot \frac{4\,p_c^2}{(p_c+p_f)^2}. \] (You can obtain these via Shephard’s lemma from the expenditure function below.)

5) Expenditure function

For CES with \(\sigma=2\) (equal weights up to a normalization consistent with the utility above), the price index is \[ \Pi(p_c,p_f)=\frac{4\,p_c p_f}{p_c+p_f}, \] and the expenditure function is \[ e(p_c,p_f,\bar u)=\bar u\,\Pi(p_c,p_f) =\bar u\cdot \frac{4\,p_c p_f}{p_c+p_f}. \] Then \(X_j^{h}=\partial e/\partial p_j\) gives the Hicksian demands in 4).

6) Marshallian = Hicksian at the Marshallian utility

Let \(X^{M}(p,I)\) be Marshallian demands and \(\bar u=U\!\big(X^{M}(p,I)\big)\) be the attained utility. Since \(e(p,\bar u)=I\) for homothetic CES, we have \[ X^{M}(p,I)=X^{h}\big(p,\bar u\big). \] Verification: Using \(I=e(p,\bar u)=\bar u\,\frac{4p_c p_f}{p_c+p_f}\) in \(X_c^{M}=\frac{I\,p_f}{p_c(p_c+p_f)}\) gives \[ X_c^{M} =\frac{\bar u\,\frac{4p_c p_f}{p_c+p_f}\,p_f}{p_c(p_c+p_f)} =\bar u\cdot \frac{4\,p_f^2}{(p_c+p_f)^2}=X_c^{h}. \] Similarly for \(X_f\). Hence Marshallian and Hicksian coincide at the Marshallian utility level.

Solution to Exercise 2

Each city starts with \((X_0,0)\) and can sell \(s\in[0,X_0]\) services for cash at net price \(0.8\,p_X\). Thus \[ X=X_0-s,\qquad C=0.8\,p_X\,s. \] Maximize \(U=\sqrt{XC}\) subject to this tradeoff. The FOC uses the MRS: \[ \text{MRS}_{X,C}=\frac{\partial U/\partial X}{\partial U/\partial C} =\frac{C}{X}. \] Optimality along the feasible line requires \[ \frac{C}{X}=0.8\,p_X \quad\Rightarrow\quad C=0.8\,p_X\,X. \] Using \(C=0.8\,p_X\,(X_0-X)\) from the tradeoff: \[ 0.8\,p_X\,(X_0-X)=0.8\,p_X\,X \;\Rightarrow\; X=\frac{X_0}{2},\quad C=0.8\,p_X\,\frac{X_0}{2}=0.4\,p_X\,X_0. \] Therefore - City 1 (\(X_0=1\)): \((X_1^*,C_1^*)=\big(\tfrac{1}{2},\,0.4\,p_X\big)\). - City 2 (\(X_0=2\)): \((X_2^*,C_2^*)=\big(1,\,0.8\,p_X\big)\).

(Contrast: without the 80% wedge you would have \(C=\tfrac{1}{2}p_X X_0\); the wedge reduces cash by 20%.)

Solution to Exercise 3

Let prices be \((p_t,p_a)\) with \(p_a=1\) (numeraire), \(p_t=p\) unless otherwise stated.

1) Marshallian demands

For \(U=x^\alpha y^{1-\alpha}\), \[ x^M=\alpha\,\frac{I}{p_x},\qquad y^M=(1-\alpha)\,\frac{I}{p_y}. \] Incomes from endowments: \(I_C=4p+1\), \(I_D=p+4\).

Hence \[ \text{Carol }(\alpha=\tfrac13):\quad X_{t,C}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{I_C}{p},\quad X_{a,C}=\frac{2}{3} I_C, \] \[ \text{Dave }(\alpha=\tfrac23):\quad X_{t,D}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{I_D}{p},\quad X_{a,D}=\frac{1}{3} I_D. \]

2) Equilibrium prices (no taxes)

Market clearing for tobacco (total endowment \(5\)): \[ \frac{1}{3}\frac{4p+1}{p}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{p+4}{p}=5 \;\Rightarrow\; p=1. \] Thus \((p_t,p_a)=(1,1)\).

3) Equilibrium allocations (no taxes)

With \(p=1\), \(I_C=I_D=5\): \[ \text{Carol:}\quad X_{t,C}=\tfrac{5}{3},\quad X_{a,C}=\tfrac{10}{3},\qquad \text{Dave:}\quad X_{t,D}=\tfrac{10}{3},\quad X_{a,D}=\tfrac{5}{3}. \] Totals: \((5,5)\) as required.

4) 20% consumption tax on tobacco

Buyers pay \((1+\tau)p_t=(1.2)p_t\) for tobacco; sellers receive \(p_t\). Keep \(p_a=1\) as numeraire.

Let the producer (seller) relative price of tobacco be \(p\) so consumers face \(p^{c}=(1+\tau)p=1.2p\). Incomes from selling endowments are at producer prices: \[ I_C=4p+1,\qquad I_D=p+4. \] With lump-sum transfers \(T_C,T_D\), consumer demands are \[ X_{t,C}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{I_C+T_C}{1.2p}, \quad X_{a,C}=\frac{2}{3}(I_C+T_C), \qquad X_{t,D}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{I_D+T_D}{1.2p}, \quad X_{a,D}=\frac{1}{3}(I_D+T_D). \] Market clearing requires \[ X_{t,C}+X_{t,D}=5,\qquad X_{a,C}+X_{a,D}=5. \] Government revenue is tax-per-unit times quantity traded: \[ R=\tau\,p\cdot Q_t=0.2\,p\cdot 5=1.0\,p. \] A revenue-neutral policy imposes \(T_C+T_D=R= p\).

(a) Equilibrium conditions (summary)

\[ \frac{(I_C+T_C)/3+2(I_D+T_D)/3}{1.2p}=5, \qquad \frac{2(I_C+T_C)+(I_D+T_D)}{3}=5, \qquad T_C+T_D=p. \]

(b) One revenue-neutral example: equal rebates \(T_C=T_D=\frac{p}{2}\)

Use the alcohol market (\(X_{a,\cdot}=5\)): \[ 2(4p+1+\tfrac{p}{2})+(p+4+\tfrac{p}{2})=15 \;\Rightarrow\; 10.5p+6=15 \;\Rightarrow\; p=\frac{6}{7}\approx 0.8571. \] - Producer tobacco price: \(p_t=p=\tfrac{6}{7}\).
- Consumer tobacco price: \(p_t^c=1.2p=\tfrac{36}{35}\approx 1.0286\).
- Transfers: \(T_C=T_D=\tfrac{p}{2}=\tfrac{3}{7}\).

Allocations. Incomes: \(I_C=\frac{31}{7}\), \(I_D=\frac{34}{7}\). Then \[ X_{t,C}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\frac{31}{7}+\frac{3}{7}}{\frac{36}{35}} =\frac{595}{756}\approx 1.482,\qquad X_{t,D}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{\frac{34}{7}+\frac{3}{7}}{\frac{36}{35}} =\frac{1295}{756}\approx 3.518, \] summing to \(5\). For alcohol: \[ X_{a,C}=\frac{2}{3}\cdot \frac{34}{7}=\frac{68}{21}\approx 3.238,\qquad X_{a,D}=\frac{1}{3}\cdot \frac{37}{7}=\frac{37}{21}\approx 1.762, \] summing to \(5\).

(c) If revenue is rebated equally

As computed above: \(T_C=T_D=\frac{p}{2}\) with \(p=\frac{6}{7}\), i.e. each receives \(\frac{3}{7}\).

Remarks.
- With a consumption tax wedge, producer and consumer prices differ.
- Lump-sum transfers are needed in pure exchange to re-spend the tax revenue; otherwise aggregate demand falls short of endowments.
- Different splits \((T_C,T_D)\) lead to different relative prices \(p\) but still clear markets (Second Welfare Theorem logic).

Solution to Exercise 4

We solve the nonlinear (block) price problem explicitly. Prices are piecewise linear per good:

  • Apples: first \(\bar A\) units at \(\bar p_a\), any additional unit at \(\bar p_a/2\).
  • Pears: first \(\bar P\) units at \(\bar p_p\), any additional unit at \(\bar p_p/2\).

Both Eve (E) and Frank (F) have \(U(X_A,X_P)=X_A^\alpha X_P^{1-\alpha}\) with \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), endowments \((\omega_{EA},\omega_{EP})\), \((\omega_{FA},\omega_{FP})\). Let totals be \[ \Omega_A=\omega_{EA}+\omega_{FA},\qquad \Omega_P=\omega_{EP}+\omega_{FP}. \]

We treat the value of any quantity \(x\) of a good at the block price as its expenditure function: \[ E_A(x)= \begin{cases} \bar p_a\,x, & 0\le x\le \bar A,\\ \bar p_a\,\bar A+\dfrac{\bar p_a}{2}(x-\bar A), & x>\bar A, \end{cases} \qquad E_P(x)= \begin{cases} \bar p_p\,x, & 0\le x\le \bar P,\\ \bar p_p\,\bar P+\dfrac{\bar p_p}{2}(x-\bar P), & x>\bar P. \end{cases} \] The inverse (quantity from a spending target \(s\) on that good) is \[ X_A(s)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{s}{\bar p_a}, & 0\le s\le \bar p_a\bar A,\\[6pt] \bar A+\dfrac{2}{\bar p_a}\big(s-\bar p_a\bar A\big), & s> \bar p_a\bar A, \end{cases} \qquad X_P(s)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{s}{\bar p_p}, & 0\le s\le \bar p_p\bar P,\\[6pt] \bar P+\dfrac{2}{\bar p_p}\big(s-\bar p_p\bar P\big), & s> \bar p_p\bar P. \end{cases} \]

Step 1 — Each consumer’s income at the block schedule

Think of “cashing out” the endowment at the same schedule (the standard approach with nonlinear prices). For \(i\in\{E,F\}\), \[ I_i \equiv E_A(\omega_{iA})+E_P(\omega_{iP}). \] Write these explicitly with the piecewise forms above (note: selling uses the same schedule).

Step 2 — Marshallian (block-price) demands for Cobb–Douglas

With Cobb–Douglas, the consumer tries to spend shares \(\alpha I_i\) on apples and \((1-\alpha)I_i\) on pears. Under the block schedule, the resulting Marshallian quantities are obtained by plugging these target spendings into the inverse maps: \[ X_{A,i}= \begin{cases} \dfrac{\alpha I_i}{\bar p_a}, & \alpha I_i\le \bar p_a\bar A,\\[6pt] \bar A+\dfrac{2}{\bar p_a}\big(\alpha I_i-\bar p_a\bar A\big), & \alpha I_i> \bar p_a\bar A, \end{cases} \qquad X_{P,i}= \begin{cases} \dfrac{(1-\alpha) I_i}{\bar p_p}, & (1-\alpha) I_i\le \bar p_p\bar P,\\[6pt] \bar P+\dfrac{2}{\bar p_p}\big((1-\alpha) I_i-\bar p_p\bar P\big), & (1-\alpha) I_i> \bar p_p\bar P. \end{cases} \]

These are closed-form individual demands at the block prices.

Step 3 — Market clearing and equilibrium prices

We choose a numeraire (only relative prices matter). Set \(\bar p_p\equiv 1\) and solve for the relative price \(\bar p_a\).

We now present a fully explicit equilibrium in the economically most relevant regime:

Regime R (deep-interior, both kinks active):
Totals are beyond thresholds, and each consumer’s target spending on each good exceeds the first block:
\[\Omega_A>\bar A,\quad \Omega_P>\bar P,\] \[\alpha I_i>\bar p_a\bar A,\quad (1-\alpha)I_i>\bar P,\qquad i\in\{E,F\}.\] (All these will be verifiable inequalities; see “Case checks” below.)

Under Regime R, everyone buys into the second block on both goods. Then \[ X_{A,i}=\bar A+\frac{2}{\bar p_a}\big(\alpha I_i-\bar p_a\bar A\big),\qquad X_{P,i}=\bar P+2\big((1-\alpha) I_i-\bar P\big), \] and aggregate demands simplify to \[ X_{A,E}+X_{A,F}=\frac{2\alpha}{\bar p_a}\,(I_E+I_F),\qquad X_{P,E}+X_{P,F}=2(1-\alpha)\,(I_E+I_F). \] Market clearing \(X_{\cdot}= \Omega_{\cdot}\) gives \[ \frac{2\alpha}{\bar p_a}(I_E+I_F)=\Omega_A,\qquad 2(1-\alpha)(I_E+I_F)=\Omega_P. \] Hence the aggregate income at block prices is pinned down by pears (the numeraire): \[ I_E+I_F=\frac{\Omega_P}{2(1-\alpha)}. \] Then the equilibrium relative price of apples is \[ \boxed{\;\bar p_a=\frac{2\alpha}{\Omega_A}\,(I_E+I_F)=\frac{2\alpha}{\Omega_A}\cdot \frac{\Omega_P}{2(1-\alpha)} =\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\cdot \frac{\Omega_P}{\Omega_A}\;} \] with \(\bar p_p\equiv 1\). (This is intuitive: a Cobb–Douglas price ratio scaled by the relative scarcity \(\Omega_P/\Omega_A\).)

To finish the equilibrium, compute individual incomes (at these prices) and then allocations.

  • With \(\bar p_p=1\) and the totals beyond thresholds, the value of the aggregate endowment is \[ I_E+I_F=E_A(\Omega_A)+E_P(\Omega_P) =\frac{\bar p_a}{2}(\Omega_A+\bar A)+\frac{1}{2}(\Omega_P+\bar P). \] Using the value forced by market clearing above is a consistency condition in Regime R and indeed holds with the \(\bar p_a\) we found.

  • Individual incomes are \[ I_i = E_A(\omega_{iA})+E_P(\omega_{iP}) = \begin{cases} \bar p_a\,\omega_{iA}, & \omega_{iA}\le \bar A,\\ \bar p_a\,\bar A+\dfrac{\bar p_a}{2}(\omega_{iA}-\bar A), & \omega_{iA}>\bar A, \end{cases} + \begin{cases} \omega_{iP}, & \omega_{iP}\le \bar P,\\ \bar P+\dfrac{1}{2}(\omega_{iP}-\bar P), & \omega_{iP}>\bar P. \end{cases} \]

  • Individual allocations (closed-form): \[ X_{A,i}=\bar A+\frac{2}{\bar p_a}\big(\alpha I_i-\bar p_a\bar A\big),\qquad X_{P,i}=\bar P+2\big((1-\alpha) I_i-\bar P\big), \] which automatically add up to \(\Omega_A,\Omega_P\) across \(i=E,F\).

Putting it all together:

Equilibrium in Regime R (deep interior, \(\bar p_p=1\)): \[ \boxed{\;\bar p_a=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\cdot \frac{\Omega_P}{\Omega_A},\qquad \bar p_p=1\;} \] \[ \boxed{\;I_i=E_A(\omega_{iA};\bar p_a,\bar A)+E_P(\omega_{iP};1,\bar P)\;} \] \[ \boxed{\;X_{A,i}=\bar A+\frac{2}{\bar p_a}\big(\alpha I_i-\bar p_a\bar A\big),\qquad X_{P,i}=\bar P+2\big((1-\alpha) I_i-\bar P\big)\;} \] with \(i\in\{E,F\}\) and \(X_{A,E}+X_{A,F}=\Omega_A\), \(X_{P,E}+X_{P,F}=\Omega_P\).

Step 4 — Case checks and alternative regimes (complete specification)

You must verify the inequalities defining Regime R:

  1. Totals beyond thresholds: \(\Omega_A>\bar A\), \(\Omega_P>\bar P\).

  2. Individual spending beyond first blocks: \[ \alpha I_i>\bar p_a\bar A,\qquad (1-\alpha)I_i>\bar P,\qquad i=E,F, \] using the \(\bar p_a\) above and the \(I_i\) computed from endowments.

  • If a condition fails for apples (say \(\alpha I_i\le \bar p_a\bar A\)), then for that \(i\) replace \[ X_{A,i}=\frac{\alpha I_i}{\bar p_a} \quad\text{(first block only)}, \] and re-clear the apple market for \(\bar p_a\) using the exact sum of individual cases (some first-block, some second-block): \[ \sum_{i:\,\alpha I_i\le \bar p_a\bar A}\frac{\alpha I_i}{\bar p_a} \;+\; \sum_{i:\,\alpha I_i> \bar p_a\bar A}\left[\bar A+\frac{2}{\bar p_a}\big(\alpha I_i-\bar p_a\bar A\big)\right] \;=\; \Omega_A. \] This is linear in \((1/\bar p_a)\) and solves in closed form. (Analogous for pears.)

  • If a total is not beyond its threshold (e.g. \(\Omega_A\le \bar A\)), then the second block is never used for that good. Set \[ X_{A,i}=\frac{\alpha I_i}{\bar p_a},\qquad \sum_i X_{A,i}=\frac{\alpha (I_E+I_F)}{\bar p_a}=\Omega_A \ \Longrightarrow\ \bar p_a=\frac{\alpha (I_E+I_F)}{\Omega_A}, \] with \(\bar p_p\) obtained analogously (and one normalized to \(1\)). Individual allocations then use the first-block formulas only.

In all regimes, the procedure is complete and constructive: compute \(I_i\) from endowments at the block prices, apply the appropriate (first/second-block) individual demand formula, and solve the two linear market-clearing equations (one per good) for \((\bar p_a,\bar p_p)\) up to a numeraire. The closed forms above cover each case.

Step 5 — Comparative statics in \(\bar A,\bar P\)

  • In Regime R, prices (with \(\bar p_p=1\)) are \[ \bar p_a=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\cdot \frac{\Omega_P}{\Omega_A}, \] which do not depend directly on \((\bar A,\bar P)\) in this deep-interior case. However, increasing \(\bar A\) or \(\bar P\) makes it easier for individuals to remain in Regime R (their “beyond-kink” inequalities \(\alpha I_i>\bar p_a\bar A\), \((1-\alpha)I_i>\bar P\) become harder to satisfy as thresholds rise), potentially pushing the economy into mixed regimes where some consumers buy only in first blocks. In those regimes, higher \(\bar A\) (or \(\bar P\)) lowers the effective marginal price schedule for larger quantities (since more units are at the higher first-block price before the half-price kicks in), which typically raises equilibrium price levels to re-clear markets.

  • If \(\Omega_A\le \bar A\) (or \(\Omega_P\le \bar P\)), the second block is irrelevant for that good; comparative statics are the standard Cobb–Douglas ones with linear prices.

Conclusion. The solution above gives closed-form individual demands at the block schedule, a closed-form price ratio (and prices up to a numeraire) in the interior regime, and a complete algorithm with explicit formulas for all alternative regimes. This fully “does” the problem rather than sketching it.

Solution to Exercise 5

1–4) Edgeworth box, indifference curves, budget slopes

  • Box is \(6\times 6\). Initial endowment at \((4,2)\) for \(A\) (hence \((2,4)\) for \(B\)).
  • Budget lines pass through the endowment with slope \(-\frac{p_1}{p_2}\).
  • Indifference curves for \(A\): \(\bar u=X_1^\alpha X_2^{1-\alpha}\Rightarrow X_2=\bar u^{1/(1-\alpha)}X_1^{-\alpha/(1-\alpha)}\) — convex to the origin (diminishing MRS).

5) Equilibrium prices and allocation

Let \(p_2=1\), \(p_1=p\). Aggregate demand for good 1: \[ X_{1,A}+X_{1,B} =\alpha\frac{I_A}{p}+\alpha\frac{I_B}{p} =\alpha\frac{p(4+2)+1(2+4)}{p} =\alpha\cdot 6\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\Big). \] Set equal to total supply \(6\): \[ \alpha\Big(1+\frac{1}{p}\Big)=1 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{p_1}{p_2}=p=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}. \] Thus the equilibrium price ratio is \(\frac{p_1}{p_2}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\).

Allocations. With \(p=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\), \[ I_A=p\cdot 4+1\cdot 2 =\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\cdot 4+2 =\frac{2}{1-\alpha},\qquad I_B=p\cdot 2+1\cdot 4 =\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\cdot 2+4 =\frac{4}{1-\alpha}. \] Then \[ X_{1,A}=\alpha\frac{I_A}{p} =\alpha\cdot \frac{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}=2,\qquad X_{2,A}=(1-\alpha)I_A=(1-\alpha)\cdot \frac{2}{1-\alpha}=2, \] \[ X_{1,B}=\alpha\frac{I_B}{p} =\alpha\cdot \frac{\frac{4}{1-\alpha}}{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}=4,\qquad X_{2,B}=(1-\alpha)I_B=(1-\alpha)\cdot \frac{4}{1-\alpha}=4. \] So the equilibrium allocation is \((X_A,X_B)=\big( (2,2), (4,4) \big)\), independent of \(\alpha\) (given the symmetric total endowment \((6,6)\) and Cobb–Douglas homotheticity).
